# Level-1 formulation

## Configuration

For body pose `T(s) ∈ SE(3)` and eight 3-DOF legs,

\[
q(s) \in \mathbb{R}^{24}, \qquad c_i(s)\in\{0,1\}.
\]

The path parameter `s` is not physical time. Contact is piecewise constant; body pose and joint coordinates are continuous in the target formulation.

## Foot kinematics

For leg `i`,

\[
p_i(T,q_i)=FK_i(T,q_i).
\]

When `c_i=1`, the foot is a fixed no-slip point contact:

\[
p_{i,z}=0, \qquad \frac{dp_i}{ds}=0.
\]

When `c_i=0`,

\[
p_{i,z}\ge 0.
\]

## Static geometric support condition

Level 1 uses the **body geometric center projection**, not COM dynamics. There must exist support weights

\[
\lambda_i\ge0,\qquad \sum_i\lambda_i=1,\qquad 0\le\lambda_i\le c_i
\]

such that

\[
p_{body}^{xy}=\sum_i \lambda_i p_i^{xy}.
\]

Equivalently, the projected body geometric center lies in the convex hull of current support feet.

## Hard constraints

The current Level-1 model contains:

- joint limits;
- support-foot fixed contact and contact height;
- swing-foot non-penetration;
- body geometric-center support-hull condition;
- body-ground non-penetration;
- link-ground non-penetration;
- self-collision / link-body checks in the independent checker.

The current search prototype intentionally does **not** reject candidates for self-collision yet; stepping/contact generation is being isolated first.

## Forward + roll benchmark task

For roll parameter `θ` in degrees,

\[
t_B(\theta)=\begin{bmatrix}\theta/300 & 0 & 0.35\end{bmatrix}^T,
\qquad R_B(\theta)=R_x(\theta).
\]

The task path is fixed for the benchmark, while contact state, touchdown legs, liftoff legs, and touchdown locations are generated by the planner.

## Solver architecture

The current baseline deliberately separates discrete and continuous decisions:

\[
\text{contact-event search}
\quad + \quad
\text{nonlinear support/swing kinematics}.
\]

Relaxing `c_i∈{0,1}` to `c_i∈[0,1]` was previously tested with a contact-implicit NLP, but fractional contact variables exploited big-M slack and did not provide a reliable binary contact selector. Therefore the baseline keeps contact decisions discrete and uses nonlinear continuous solves inside each candidate branch.

At a stalled support mode, candidate `m` is ranked approximately by

\[
S_m = \Delta\theta_{lookahead}
      -0.12\,N_{switch}
      +0.03\,N_{support}.
\]

This score is a search heuristic; Level-1 validity remains a hard feasibility requirement rather than a penalty.
